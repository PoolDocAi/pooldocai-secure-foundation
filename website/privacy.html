Privacy Policy

Draft — formal legal review required. This document is a working disclosure for product development and is not a completed legal compliance review. The final policy must confirm the legal operator, jurisdictions, vendors, retention periods, individual rights, transfer practices, and business contact details before commercial launch.

This draft describes the categories of information PoolDocAI is designed to process and why. It should be read with the Terms of Use draft and any company-specific data-processing agreement or configuration.

1. Information we may process

  • Account and company information: user and company identifiers, role and seat permissions, invitations, authentication status, and account configuration.
  • Troubleshooting content: messages, equipment category and model details, conversation context, diagnostic steps, citations, user feedback, and service summaries.
  • Documents and source material: uploaded files or imported public webpages, extracted text, chunks, embeddings, indexing status, document rights declarations, and company-selected sharing preferences.
  • Service requests: contact details or service location entered by a requester, issue summaries, routing status, and notification-delivery metadata.
  • Usage, security, and technical information: requests, quota and cost-metering events, timestamps, errors, limited session digests, page analytics, and information needed to reduce abuse and troubleshoot failures.

2. How information is used

Information may be used to authenticate accounts, enforce tenant and seat permissions, answer troubleshooting questions, retrieve relevant manuals, research public manufacturer sources, process documents, generate service summaries, route a request to the selected company, operate usage and cost controls, improve reliability, investigate abuse, and maintain human-reviewed learning candidates when enabled.

3. Company data isolation and learning

Company manuals and records are intended to remain tenant-private and are not automatically shared with other companies. A company’s manuals, SOPs, safety policies, and private operating procedures must remain private. General learning is a separate opt-in that requires an explicit rights attestation and platform-owner review before a candidate may be approved for shared retrieval. An item entering a review queue is not automatically published and does not itself fine-tune a model. The final policy must describe any external model provider’s processing and any permitted data-retention or training settings precisely.

4. Service providers and public research

PoolDocAI is designed to use hosted infrastructure, a database/storage service, an AI model provider, and public-web research sources when manuals are insufficient. The final policy must identify the providers actually enabled in production, the purposes for which data is sent to each, applicable contractual terms, and whether a given provider receives message text, extracted source text, images, identifiers, or only a limited query. Do not assume that every configured or future integration is currently active.

5. Public demo and consent

The public demo requests affirmative acceptance of the current Terms and Privacy notice before sending demo troubleshooting turns and may save redacted diagnostic notes with a one-way session digest. It is not designed to store raw browser session tokens in the learning-event record. Do not enter customer names, addresses, phone numbers, access codes, payment details, or other unnecessary personal information. A visitor-reported resolution may create a pending human-review candidate; it is not automatically approved or used to train a model.

6. Security and access

PoolDocAI uses authentication, tenant-specific authorization, row-level access controls, server-side secrets, and private document storage as part of its intended security design. No internet-connected system can be promised perfectly secure. Users should use strong credentials, restrict access to authorized team members, avoid sharing access tokens, and report suspected unauthorized access promptly.

7. Retention, deletion, and individual choices

Information is retained as needed to provide service features, maintain auditability and security, enforce company entitlements, and support configured diagnostic or document workflows. Exact retention periods, deletion timelines, backup handling, legal holds, account closure effects, and jurisdiction-specific request procedures have not yet been finalized in this draft and must be confirmed before commercial launch. Company administrators should not upload information unless they are authorized to direct its processing.

8. Cookies and analytics

The service may use browser storage to maintain public-demo session limits and consent state, and may use website analytics to measure page usage and improve the user experience. The final policy must enumerate active cookies, local/session-storage identifiers, analytics providers, consent controls, and any regional notice requirements after the production configuration is confirmed.

9. Children and sensitive information

PoolDocAI is a technical support product for adults, homeowners, service professionals, and businesses, not a service designed for children. Do not enter sensitive personal information or information about another person unless you have a lawful basis and appropriate authorization. Final age restrictions and privacy protections are subject to legal review.

10. Contact and changes

A dedicated privacy-request email address and mailing address have not yet been published. The final policy must provide valid contact details, identify the legal operator and relevant jurisdictions, set the effective date, and explain material updates and individual rights before commercial launch. Until a dedicated channel is announced, use the Early Access page only for ordinary inquiries.
